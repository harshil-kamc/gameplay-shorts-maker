# Skill: Hooks

The first 1–3 seconds. For gameplay shorts, the hook must be VISIBLE, not just spoken.

**Proven gameplay hook patterns** (pick the one the moment naturally fits — never force one):
- **Chaos cold-open:** drop straight into the crash, firefight, chase, or collapse.
- **Near-death save:** open at 1 HP, the falling vehicle, the edge of the cliff, or the boss wind-up.
- **Big fail / big win:** start on the ridiculous outcome, then let the clip show how it happened.
- **Stunt / set piece:** lead with the most cinematic moving frame, not the setup.
- **Glitch / absurdity:** open on the impossible thing the game just did.
- **Curiosity question:** "How did he survive that?" / "Would you drive this in Vice City?"
- **Number / stakes:** "One health bar. One boss. No checkpoint."

**Rules:**
- Lead with the payoff frame, not the windup. Cut the "watch this", "here we go", or menu preamble.
- The on-screen text hook and the visible action should reinforce each other.
- Front-load motion or impact in the first frame.
- If the strongest action is 10s in, START there and let context come after.
- Avoid generic openers: "In this video…", "Today we're playing…", greetings.

**Gameplay-short evidence:**
- Winning gameplay hooks stop the scroll with motion in frame 1 — impact, danger, speed, or a HUD state that needs explanation.
- Titles that promise a concrete event ("FLIPS A CAR INTO THE BOSS ARENA") beat vague personality-driven titles.
- 20-45s wins: one complete action beat with a clean payoff. Sub-15s clips collapsed in reach.
- Top comments ask "how?" or argue about the play — end on the moment or a question, not a summary.
